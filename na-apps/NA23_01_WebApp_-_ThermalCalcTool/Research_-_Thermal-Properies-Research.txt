### Primary Thermal Data for U-Value Calculations in UK Construction

### Introduction
- This report provides a robust dataset of thermal properties (λ-values) for UK construction materials
- Data supports U-value calculations for UK Building Regulations compliance
- Specifically designed for an automated web application for architects

### Importance of Accurate Thermal Data
- U-value calculations are fundamental to Part L compliance (Conservation of Fuel and Power)
- Inaccurate data leads to:
  - Significant miscalculations of building fabric heat loss
  - Non-compliance with energy performance standards
  - Performance gaps between design and actual energy use
  - Potential legal liabilities for designers and developers
- Data integrity is critical for automated calculation applications

### Hierarchy and Nature of Sources
- Data sourced from authoritative UK industry documents and standards
- Primary sources in order of preference:
  - Official UK Guidance and Standards:
    - BRE Report BR 443 (2019): "Conventions for U-value calculations"
    - CIBSE Guide A: "Environmental Design"
    - BS EN ISO 10456: Hygrothermal properties standards
    - BS EN 12524: Tabulated design values
  - Third-Party Certifications:
    - British Board of Agrément (BBA) certificates
    - λ90/90 values for enhanced reliability
  - Manufacturer-Specific Technical Datasheets:
    - Cross-referenced with BBA certification
    - Verified against BS EN product standards

### Scope of Materials Covered
- Thermal insulation (PIR, phenolic foam, mineral wool, glass wool, XPS, EPS)
- Masonry (clay bricks, concrete blocks, stone)
- Concrete and Screeds
- Timber and Wood-Based Products
- Roofing Materials
- Plasters, Renders, and Boards
- Ancillary materials and air layers

### Units and Definitions
- Thermal Conductivity (λ):
  - Units: Watts per metre Kelvin (W/mK)
  - Represents material's heat conduction ability
  - Lower λ = better insulation
- Thermal Resistance (R):
  - Units: square metres Kelvin per Watt (m²K/W)
  - Formula: R = thickness/λ
- Declared Thermal Conductivity (λD or λ90/90):
  - Product-specific value under reference conditions
  - 90% confidence that 90% of production meets/exceeds value
  - Statistically robust for design calculations

### 2. Thermal Properties of Common UK Insulation Materials
- Thermal performance data is critical for U-value calculations
- Declared thermal conductivity (λD or λ90/90) values from:
  - BBA Certificates
  - Manufacturer technical datasheets (referencing BBA certification)
  - Testing to BS EN product standards (BS EN 13162 to BS EN 13171)
- BR 443:2019 recommends direct manufacturer data sourcing
- Foamed plastics declared values represent 25-year average performance

- High-performance insulation (PIR, Phenolic foams):
  - Data primarily from manufacturer-specific BBA certificates
  - Proprietary formulations affect thermal performance
  - Generic "book values" less reliable
  - Declared values (λD or λ90/90) provide assurance for:
    - Ageing effects in foams
    - Manufacturing consistency
    - Calculation accuracy
    - Liability concerns

- Thickness-dependent thermal conductivity:
  - Some insulation boards show varying λ values with thickness
  - Related to manufacturing processes and surface effects
  - Single lambda value may not be accurate for entire product range
  - Thickness-specific values should be used when available

- BBA λ90/90 values provide:
  - 90% confidence that 90% of production meets/exceeds performance
  - More conservative and dependable figures
  - Reduced gap between design and as-built performance

### 8. Thermal Properties of Ancillary Materials

### 8.1. Vapour Control Layers and Breather Membranes

- Material thermal resistance generally negligible in standard calculations
- Primary functions: moisture control and airtightness
- Typical thicknesses: 0.20mm to 0.5mm
- Reflective surfaces can enhance adjacent air cavity resistance
- Low-emissivity surfaces (ε < 0.2) reduce radiative heat transfer
- BBA Info Bulletin No.5: Reflective breather membrane (ε 0.05) can increase cavity resistance from 0.18 to 0.67 m²K/W
- Proper installation crucial for performance
- Must maintain intended unventilated air space
- Follow manufacturer and industry guidance (NHBC, TRADA)

### 8.2. Air Layers/Cavities

- Thermal resistance depends on:
  - Thickness (optimal ~25mm for vertical cavities)
  - Orientation (vertical/horizontal/sloped)
  - Ventilation level
  - Surface emissivities
- Unventilated airspaces offer highest resistance
- Slightly ventilated airspaces have reduced resistance
- Well-ventilated spaces often treated as external surface
- High emissivity surfaces (>0.8) result in lower R-values
- Detailed guidance in BR 443:2019 and BS EN ISO 6946

### 9.4. BS EN 12524

- European standard for hygrothermal properties
- Provides tabulated design values
- Widely adopted in national documents

- Key European reference for generic thermal data

### 9.5. BBA Certificates

- Independent third-party certification
- Verifies fitness-for-purpose including thermal performance
- Primary source for insulation product data
- Values determined through standardised testing
- Essential for compliance demonstration
- Website allows certificate verification

### 9.6. Manufacturer Technical Datasheets

- Direct product information source
- Should reference relevant BS/EN standards

### 10. Standards Interconnection

- BR 443 applies conventions to U-value calculations per BS EN ISO 6946
- Thermal conductivity data should follow BS EN ISO 10456 or EN 12524
- BBA certification provides essential verification layer for manufacturer claims

### 11. Key Data Considerations

- Standardised Data Priority:
  - Use values from BS EN ISO 10456 and BS EN 12524
  - Follow UK conventions (BR 443:2019, CIBSE Guide A)
  - Ensure consistency and defensibility

- Insulation Data Sources:
  - BBA Certificates and manufacturer datasheets for proprietary products
  - Account for product-specific formulations and ageing factors
  - Avoid generic "book values" for high-performance materials

- Material Properties:
  - Density and moisture content affect thermal conductivity
  - Higher values increase conductivity
  - Allow parameter selection or use conservative UK values

### 12. Critical Calculation Conventions

- Thin membranes (VCLs, breather membranes):
  - Negligible thermal resistance
  - Reflective surfaces enhance adjacent air cavity resistance

- Roof coverings:
  - Neglect thermal resistance in pitched, ventilated constructions
  - Account for thermal bridging per BS EN ISO 6946 and BR 443

## Introduction to Insulated Plasterboard

### Definition
- Composite building product
- Factory-bonded thermal insulation to plasterboard
- 2-in-1 solution (dry lining + insulation)
- Optional 3-in-1 with vapour control layer (VCL)

### Key Benefits
- Reduced installation time
- Lower labor costs
- Suitable for new build and renovation
- Enhanced thermal performance
- Improved energy efficiency
- Reduced carbon footprint
- Better thermal comfort
- Reduced condensation risk
- Potential sound insulation benefits

### Common Applications
- Internal wall insulation (IWI)
- Roof insulation
- Ceiling linings
- New build projects
- Refurbishment projects
### Common Applications

#### Internal Wall Insulation (IWI)
- Primary use: Upgrading thermal performance of existing solid masonry walls
- Suitable for UK's older housing stock
- Used for cavity wall internal insulation
- Compatible with timber and steel-framed constructions

#### Roof Insulation
- Pitched roofs: Insulation at rafter level
- Common in room-in-roof and loft conversions
- Flat roofs: Installation beneath joists

#### Ceiling Linings
- Thermal insulation for ceilings
- Used beneath uninsulated lofts
- Improves insulation between storeys

#### New Build and Refurbishment
- New builds: Helps meet Part L Building Regulations
- Refurbishment: Practical thermal upgrade solution

### Installation Considerations
- Dual functionality: Insulation + dry lining finish
- Critical moisture management requirements
- VCL effectiveness depends on proper installation
- Key installation aspects:
  - Joint sealing between boards
  - Perimeter sealing
  - Service penetration sealing
- Poor installation can lead to:
  - VCL discontinuity
  - Interstitial condensation
  - Altered thermal dynamics
  - Dew point location issues

### Insulation Core Materials

#### Phenolic Foam (PF)
- Rigid thermoset insulation
- High closed-cell content
- Fine cellular structure
- Low-emissivity composite foil facings
- Fibre-free material

##### Performance Characteristics
- Thermal: λ = 0.018-0.021 W/mK
- Fire: Euroclass B-s1,d0 (e.g., Kingspan Kooltherm K118)
- Moisture: Good resistance with effective VCL

#### Polyisocyanurate (PIR)
- Rigid thermoset foam
- High closed-cell content
- Fibre-free
- Low-emissivity foil or paper facings

##### Performance Characteristics
- Thermal: λ ≈ 0.022 W/mK
- Fire: Euroclass B-s1,d0
- Moisture: Good resistance with effective VCL
- Note: Potential toxic smoke release during combustion

#### Expanded Polystyrene (EPS)
- Lightweight rigid foam
- Expanded polystyrene beads
- CFC/HCFC-free manufacturing

##### Performance Characteristics
- Thermal: λ = 0.037-0.040 W/mK
- Fire: Euroclass B-s1,d0 (with plasterboard)
- Moisture: Moderate resistance, variable VCL performance

#### Extruded Polystyrene (XPS)
- Rigid foam insulation with closed-cell structure
- Manufactured through extrusion process
- CFC and HCFC-free
- Higher density and compressive strength than EPS

##### Performance Characteristics
- Thermal: λ ≈ 0.033 W/mK
- Fire: Euroclass B-s1,d0 (with plasterboard)
- Moisture: Excellent water and vapour resistance
- Note: Good moisture management in composite boards

#### Gypsum Fibreboard (e.g., Fermacell)
- High-density building board
- Homogeneous mix of recycled materials:
  - Gypsum
  - Cellulose fibres
  - Water
- Superior impact and acoustic properties

##### Performance Characteristics
- Thermal: λ ≈ 0.32 W/mK
- Note: Not a direct equivalent to foam insulation
- Primary focus on structural performance
- Distinct from foam-backed systems
