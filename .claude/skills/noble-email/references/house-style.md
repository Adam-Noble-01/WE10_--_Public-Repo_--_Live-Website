# Noble Architecture — House Style for Emails and Letters

## Voice

Formal, direct, and confident without being stiff. Adam is a sole practitioner
writing to professionals and to homeowners. The register is that of a chartered
professional who knows the rules and expects to be taken at his word.

**Do:**
- State the position, then the evidence for it. "The extension eaves sits 50 mm
  below the eaves of the existing dwellinghouse and does not exceed it,
  satisfying Class A.1(d)."
- Use the passive sparingly and deliberately, usually to keep the focus on the
  scheme rather than the author.
- Be explicit about what you want the reader to do, and by when.
- Close a loop firmly when the appointment ends. Politeness does not require
  leaving a door open you intend to shut.

**Don't:**
- "Just", "quickly", "pop", "reach out", "touch base", "circle back".
- "Please don't hesitate to contact me" — say who to contact and why.
- Exclamation marks. Emoji. Rhetorical questions.
- Apologising for asking for something you are entitled to ask for.
- Hedging a fact you have verified. If the drawing proves it, assert it.

## British English and conventions

| Item | House form |
|---|---|
| Spelling | Organise, recognise, programme, enquiry, licence (noun) |
| Date in prose | `18 September 2026` |
| Date beside a recipient | `Rushcliffe Borough Council - 18`<sup>`th`</sup>` September 2026` |
| Date in a filename | `18-Sep-2026` |
| Date on a form field | `18/09/2026` |
| Money | `£1,225` — no decimals unless pence are material |
| Dimensions on drawings | `3,150 mm` |
| Dimensions in letters and forms | `3.150 m` |
| Telephone | `+44 7707 445405` |
| Time | `12:16`, 24-hour |
| Sign-off | `Yours faithfully,` to "Dear Sir or Madam"; `Yours sincerely,` to a named person |

Never mix the `mm` and `m` conventions within one document. A letter uses metres
throughout; a drawing uses millimetres throughout. They must describe the same
number.

## Structure

Letters are numbered by section with short bold headings. Sections earn their
number by being separately actionable or separately referenceable.

A typical submission letter:

```
1. Purpose of this submission
2. The application form
3. Enclosures
4. Application fee and the reference we require
5. Correspondence after submission
```

Rules:
- Section headings are sentence case, bold, in the heading navy.
- Number sequentially with no gaps. If a section is cut, **renumber** and update
  every cross-reference in the body text.
- One idea per paragraph. Three to five lines is the target.
- Anything the reader must act on gets its own labelled item — `(a)`, `(b)` —
  and is coloured red so it survives a skim.
- Anything that is reference data — dimensions, enclosures, contact details —
  goes in a table or a panel, never in a run-on sentence.

## Cross-references

Internal references to sections and note codes (`see item 5 below`,
`notes PD01 to PD10`, `Section 8 of the enclosed form`) must be correct at the
moment of sending. When a section is added or cut, re-read every cross-reference
in the letter. A letter that points at a section which no longer exists, or at a
note code that has been renumbered, undermines the whole document.

## Tone by recipient

**Local planning authority / building control.** Formal. Cite the source for
every claim. Make the ask unmissable. Assume the reader is processing fifty
applications and will skim. Never speculate about whether they will approve.

**Client.** Warmer, still precise. Explain what has happened, what happens next,
and what if anything they need to do. Avoid jargon or define it in the same
sentence. Never leave a fee or a deadline ambiguous.

**Contractor.** Brief and instructional. Lead with the change or the
requirement. Reference the drawing and revision that governs.

## Closing an appointment

When a fixed appointment ends at a milestone, say so plainly and name the person
who takes over, with their contact details in a panel. Do not soften it into
ambiguity — the point is that the recipient knows, in writing, who to contact.

The pattern that works:

> My appointment in this matter was limited to the preparation and submission of
> this application, and it concludes upon submission. I have acted as agent for
> that purpose only.
>
> Accordingly, please direct **all further correspondence to the applicant** from
> this point forward. This includes, without limitation: [list the specific
> categories].
>
> [contact panel]
>
> Please record [name] as the primary contact for this application on your
> system. Any queries, objections, requests or issues arising in respect of this
> application should be raised with him directly, and not with this practice.
>
> Should any matter arise that requires further professional input, [name] is of
> course free to re-engage this practice under a separate appointment, and any
> such instruction would need to come from him.

The last paragraph matters: it closes the loop without burning the relationship.

## Asking for something and meaning it

When Adam needs a specific thing back, and only that thing:

> So that he is able to do so without delay, I would ask that you please provide
> me by return with:
>
> (a) the application reference number allocated to this submission; and
> (b) the name, direct telephone number and email address of the officer or team
>     he should call to make payment.
>
> I would be grateful if this could be sent to me by reply to this email. I will
> then pass both items directly to Mr [name], after which he will deal with the
> Council himself. This single reply is all that I require, and I would ask that
> it not be treated as an invitation to continue corresponding with me
> thereafter.

Note the construction: the ask is numbered and red, the reason is given, and the
scope of the reply is bounded explicitly.
