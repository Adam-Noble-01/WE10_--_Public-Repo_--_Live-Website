# Common Failure Modes — the traps that catch experienced people

Each of these has a citation. Check them explicitly rather than trusting that
the obvious reading is right.

---

## 1. Measuring from the existing house instead of the original

**The trap.** Every cumulative allowance in Part 1 measures from the **original**
dwellinghouse — as it stood on **1 July 1948**, or as built if built later
(art 2 `original`). A house extended in 1998 has already spent allowance.

**Where it bites.** A.1(f)/(g) rear projection · A.1(h) two-storey projection ·
A.1(j)(iii) half-width · A.1(ja) cumulative · A.1(b) and E.1(b) 50% curtilage ·
B.1(d) roof volume via B.3 · C.1(b)/(c) original roof plane.

**But note the exceptions** — A.1(c) and A.1(d) measure against the **existing**
house, and B.1(b) against the **existing** roof. The Order alternates
deliberately. Read the sub-paragraph.

**What to do.** Ask for the planning history. Without it: CANNOT DETERMINE.

---

## 2. Treating "highway" as meaning an adopted road

**The trap.** Part 1 Interpretation: *"'highway' includes an unadopted street or
a private way"*, and `private way` (art 2) means a highway not maintainable at
public expense **and any other way other than a highway**.

**Where it bites.** A.1(e)(ii) side elevations · D.1(d) porch 2 m ·
F.2(a) driveway drainage · G.1(c) flues · H.1(e) antennas ·
Part 2 A.1(a) fence heights.

**What to do.** Ask what every bounding way is. A shared private drive counts.

---

## 3. Missing that the roof is a separate Class

**The trap.** A.1(k)(iv) removes *"an alteration to any part of the roof"* from
Class A. A rear extension that ties into the existing roof slope is Class A
**and** Class B or C.

**Where it bites hardest.** Article 2(3) land, where **Class B is unavailable
entirely** (B.1(f)). The extension can be fine and the roof tie-in fatal.

**What to do.** Look at the roof plan on every scheme, not just loft conversions.
Guidance pp. 8–9.

---

## 4. Assuming the 2019 guidance is current

**The trap.** The technical guidance was last updated 10 September 2019. It has
no Class AA, no Part 20 exclusions, a short change-of-use list, and different
paragraph numbering in places. See `00__SOURCE-REGISTER.md` §3 for the verified
list.

**What to do.** Statute first, guidance second, always.

---

## 5. Article 2(3) land ≠ the E.2 designations

**The trap.** Two different geographic tests sit side by side:

| Test | Applies in |
|---|---|
| **article 2(3) land** (Sch 1 Pt 1) | conservation area · AONB · s.41(3) WCA area · the Broads · National Park · WHS |
| **E.2** (10 m² beyond 20 m) | AONB · the Broads · National Park · WHS — **conservation areas are not included** |

**What to do.** Name the actual designation before citing either.

---

## 6. Reading the flue limit as "up to 1 metre"

**The trap.** G.1(b) bars a flue that *"would exceed the highest part of the roof
by 1 metre or more"*. Exactly 1 m **fails**. The permitted maximum is anything
**less than** 1 m.

**What to do.** Quote the sub-paragraph rather than paraphrasing it. Several
statutory limits are drafted as exclusions, so "not permitted if it exceeds X"
and "not permitted if it is X or more" mean different things at the boundary.

---

## 7. The 50% curtilage test double-counted or under-counted

**The trap.** A.1(b) and E.1(b) are the same test seen from two Classes. Both
measure **all** buildings in the curtilage other than the original house,
against 50% of the curtilage area **excluding the original house footprint**.

**What people get wrong.** Forgetting existing sheds and garages; including the
original house footprint in the numerator; using the plot area instead of the
curtilage; forgetting that fences and walls are **excluded** (art 2 `building`).

**What to do.** Show the arithmetic in the appraisal. If the curtilage area is
not given, ask.

---

## 8. Calling a Class A larger extension "needs planning permission"

**The trap.** An 8 m / 6 m rear extension under A.1(g) **is** permitted
development. It requires the A.4 neighbour consultation procedure, not a planning
application. Reporting it as "needs permission" costs the client a fee and
months.

**The mirror trap.** Reporting it as plain PD, so the client builds without
serving the A.4 notice — which makes the works unlawful.

**What to do.** Use the verdict **PERMITTED DEVELOPMENT SUBJECT TO PRIOR
APPROVAL**, and set out the A.4(2) submission requirements and the 42-day rule.

---

## 9. Assuming Class AA works like Class A's prior approval

**The trap.** Class AA has **no deemed consent**. AA.3(13) requires **written
prior approval** before works begin. There is no 42-day fallback.

**Also:** Class AA's side-elevation rule is an **outright prohibition on windows**
(AA.2(2)(b)), not an obscure-glazing condition; and its roof-pitch condition is
**"the same as"** (AA.2(2)(c)), not "so far as practicable".

---

## 10. Forgetting that Class AA extinguishes Class B

B.1(h): Class B is unavailable once the house has been enlarged in reliance on
Class AA. Adding a storey today removes the right to a dormer tomorrow.
Say so before the client commits.

---

## 11. The annexe that is really a dwelling

E.1(i) bars a building that *"relates to a dwelling"*. A garden building with a
kitchen, bathroom and bedroom, occupied independently, is a dwelling — not an
incidental building — however it is labelled on the drawing.

**What to do.** Ask about intended use and occupation. Do not decide it from room
names. Guidance p.46.

---

## 12. Render in a conservation area

A.2(a) bars cladding *"any part of the exterior"* in stone, artificial stone,
pebble dash, **render**, timber, plastic or tiles on article 2(3) land. A
rendered extension in a conservation area is not permitted development, at any
size.

---

## 13. Treating PD as the end of the matter

Permitted development is planning permission only. Listed building consent,
building regulations, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996, protected species, highways
consent, TPOs and covenants all survive. Guidance p.4 reserves the first three
expressly.

---

## 14. The unlawful existing building

art 3(5): no PD attaches where the building operations that created the existing
building were unlawful, or the existing use is unlawful. An extension to an
unlawful extension inherits nothing.

---

## 15. Conditions on a planning permission

art 3(4): nothing in the Order permits development **contrary to a condition**
on a planning permission. Modern estates and barn conversions routinely have PD
rights stripped by condition. It is invisible on the drawings and fatal to the
appraisal.

**What to do.** Ask for every decision notice, not just the most recent.

---

## 16. Margins under 100 mm

A 3.98 m extension against a 4 m limit, or a 2.49 m garden room against a 2.5 m
cap, is a dispute waiting to happen: build tolerance, ground level variation and
survey error all run in the wrong direction.

**What to do.** Flag any margin under 100 mm explicitly and recommend a **Lawful
Development Certificate** under s.192 TCPA 1990. Guidance p.4 points to the LDC
route where there is any doubt.
