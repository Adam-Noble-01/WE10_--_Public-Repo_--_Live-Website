# Critical Checks — Classes B and C: roof additions and roof alterations

Statute: `source/GPDO-2015__Sch2-Part-1__Householder.md` — Class B line 451,
Class C line 539. Guidance: pp. 33–39 (diagrams on 36, 38).

## Which one?

| | Class B | Class C |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | An addition or alteration that **enlarges** the house | **Any other** alteration to the roof |
| Typical | Dormer, hip-to-gable, roof enlargement | Rooflights, re-roofing, re-tilling, vents |
| Height measured against | the **EXISTING** roof (B.1(b)) | the **ORIGINAL** roof (C.1(c)) |
| Article 2(3) land | **Not available at all** (B.1(f)) | Available |
| Volume allowance | 40 m³ / 50 m³ | none |

That existing-vs-original distinction is real and deliberate — the guidance
flags it at p.39. Get it wrong and the answer flips.

| B-02 | No part to exceed the height of the highest part of the **EXISTING** roof | | B.1(b) |
| B-03 | Nothing beyond the plane of any **existing roof slope forming the principal elevation and fronting a highway** | Both limbs must be met for the bar to apply | B.1(c) |
| B-04 | **Cubic content of the resulting roof space** | ≤ **+40 m³** terrace house · ≤ **+50 m³** any other case | **B.1(d)** |
| B-05 | No verandah, balcony or raised platform | | B.1(e)(i) |
| B-06 | No chimney, flue or soil and vent pipe | → Class G | B.1(e)(ii) |
| B-07 | **Not on article 2(3) land** | Conservation area, AONB, National Park, the Broads, WHS → **Class B unavailable entirely** | **B.1(f)** |
| B-08 | Not a Part 20 house | | B.1(g) |
| B-09 | **House not already enlarged under Class AA** | Upward extension extinguishes Class B | **B.1(h)** |

**B-04 arithmetic.** "Resulting roof space" means the roof space *as enlarged,
taking into account any enlargement to the original roof space, whether
permitted by this Class or not* (B.3). So:

> allowance = 40 or 50 m³ − (volume of every previous roof enlargement anywhere
> on the house, measured from the **original** roof space)

`cubic content` is **measured externally** (art 2). A previous dormer on the
other side of the roof still counts. If the planning history is not supplied,
this is **CANNOT DETERMINE**.

| # | Condition | Detail | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| B-20 | Materials of similar appearance to the existing house exterior | | B.2(a) |
| B-21 | **Eaves of the original roof maintained or reinstated** | *Except* hip-to-gable, or an enlargement joining the original roof to the roof of a rear or side extension | B.2(b)(i)(aa) |
| B-22 | **0.2 m set-back** — the edge of the enlargement closest to the eaves to be not less than 0.2 m from the eaves, **measured along the roof slope from the outside edge of the eaves**, so far as practicable | Same exceptions as B-21 | **B.2(b)(i)(bb)** |
| B-23 | **No part of the enlargement beyond the outside face of any external wall of the ORIGINAL house** | *Except* an enlargement joining the original roof to a rear or side extension roof | **B.2(b)(ii)** |
| B-24 | Side-elevation windows obscure-glazed **and** non-opening below 1.7 m above the room floor | wall **or roof slope** | B.2(c) |

**B-23 relief (B.4):** roof tiles, guttering, fascias, barge boards and other
minor roof details overhanging the external wall are **not** part of the
enlargement. And "rear or side extension" includes an original part of, or a
later extension of, the house extending from the rear or side of the principal
part.

| # | Check | Limit | Fails under |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-01 | Not created by Part 3 change of use | | C.1(a) |

**Class C has no article 2(3) land bar** — rooflights in a conservation area can
be permitted development under Class C, where Class B would be unavailable.
Check for an article 4 direction, which frequently targets exactly this.

## The trap that catches everyone

A rear extension whose roof **ties into the existing roof slope** is doing two
separate things:

1. the enlargement at ground level → **Class A**; and
2. the alteration to the existing roof → **Class B or Class C**.

A.1(k)(iv) expressly removes *"an alteration to any part of the roof of the
dwellinghouse"* from Class A. So the roof element must stand on its own under B
or C. On article 2(3) land, where Class B is unavailable, this is often what
turns an otherwise-compliant scheme into a planning application.

The guidance makes the point at **pp. 8–9**: where a two-storey rear extension
has a roof joining the main roof, the works must meet the requirements of both
Classes.
